Playing optimal games of Taxman

Taxman is played on the numbers 1 to n: you take a number, and the taxman takes all of its divisors still in the pot. We found the best possible game for every n in that range, and proved each one optimal. Try game 10 first.

The proofs work in a model of the game that is exact given one statement, open in general; for the games at the start of the chain, a second program proves the scores on the whole board. What that means.

The idea in three steps

  1. Sets, not orders

    A game's score is the sum of the numbers you take, whatever their order. So the question is which numbers to take: which sets can be played at all.

  2. Frames

    The numbers fall into frames: nearly independent small games, each a few large numbers with the smaller ones that can pay for them.

  3. A few promotions

    The hard part is choosing a few factors to promote, played as moves one frame forward. A search settles which.
