Glossary

The words of this site, each with its meaning and an example. Each idea has one name.

back move
One of the two steps of peel.
board
The numbers 1 to n of game n.
bottom half
The numbers up to n/2. Each divides some larger number on the board, so a move can take it as tax, and any of them but 1 can also be a move. On the board of game 21 the bottom half is 1 to 10.

The bottom half of game n is 1 to ⌊n/2⌋.

bound
An upper limit on the sum of the promotions of any game in a node’s branch, counting those already made. The search drops a node whose bound cannot beat the best game it has found.
branch and bound
A way of searching that splits on one choice at a time, keeps the best answer found so far, and drops every branch whose bound cannot beat it.
candidate
A factor that its frame can spare and the frame in front of it can take on. Game 21 has the candidates 4 and 9.
ceiling
n more than the best score of game n − 1. No score in game n can beat it, so a game that reaches it is optimal.

The ceiling is opt(n − 1) + n.

chain
The games from game 1 up, which we solve in order, each from an optimal game of the one before.
closed
A frame with as many factors as moves, so it has none to spare. It takes no part in the search.
cofactor
n divided by its largest prime factor: 30 = 6 · 5 has the cofactor 6. One can usually tell from it whether a game needs a search.
conflict
A few candidates that the player cannot promote all together at a node. Any set the player can promote leaves at least one of them out, so the search splits on one of them.
descending greedy
The pass that chooses the sources. From the largest down, it keeps each source that is still playable with the ones it has kept.
divisor, proper divisor
A divisor of a number divides it with nothing left over. A proper divisor is one smaller than the number itself: the proper divisors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. In Taxman, a divisor is always a proper one.
exchange property
What makes a greedy choice safe in general. Whenever one playable set is smaller than another, some member of the larger one can join the smaller one, and the result is still playable. The playable sets of sources lack it in game 2,873.
fits
n fits into an optimal set of game n − 1 when the set is still playable with n added. Then game n is at its ceiling: 10 fits into game 9's optimal set, and 12 does not fit into game 11's.
forest
Numbers and the maximal factors they could pay with, with lines between them and no loop among them. Numbers with two such factors each are playable exactly when they form a forest with those factors. 10, 15 and 21 alone make 2 – 10 – 5 – 15 – 3 – 21 – 7, with no loop, and they are playable: 10, then 15, then 21.
frame
A group of sources with every factor that can pay for them, a small game inside the big one. Game 21 has four. We number the frames with freedoms from the front, frame 0 first: in game 21, frame 0 has 14, 15 and 21, and frame 1 has 12, 18 and 20. In a larger game, we collapse frames with freedoms at the same distance from the front into a single frame, with one number.

A frame is a connected component of the graph that joins each source to its maximal factors.

frame model
The game as frames, with U* as its sources, so that the promotions are all that remains to choose. Its best score is the best score of the game when statement U holds.
frame solver
The program that finds the optimal games, by searching the frame model.
Franklín and Moniot's bound
An upper bound on opt(n), the heaviest set whose numbers can each take a maximal factor of their own, from outside the set, to pay with.
freedom
A factor that a frame can spare. Each move of a frame needs a factor of its own to pay for it, so a frame has as many freedoms as it has factors beyond its moves: frame 0 of game 21 has four factors and three moves, and one freedom. A frame with none is closed.

A frame's freedoms are its factors less its moves.

front move
One of the two steps of peel.
game n
Taxman played on the numbers 1 to n. Game 21 is Taxman on 1 to 21. A game also means one play of it, as in “an optimal game”.
gap
How far a bound is above the best game the search has found. The larger the gap, the larger the search.
heaviest
With the largest weight. An optimal set is a heaviest playable set.
in front of
Frame A is in front of frame B when some number of B has a maximal factor in A, as 9 has 3 in game 21. A game plays the frames in front first, and in the figures they sit to the right.
layer
A number's count of prime factors, with repeats. 12 = 2 · 2 · 3 is in layer 3, every prime is in layer 1, and 1 alone is in layer 0.

The layer of x is Ω(x), its prime factors counted with multiplicity.

longest game
A game with the most moves its board allows. An optimal game is usually one, but not in games 833 to 835.
loop
A ring of numbers and the maximal factors they could pay with, with a line from each to the next, that comes back to where it started. 10, 15, 21 and 14 make one with their primes: 2 – 10 – 5 – 15 – 3 – 21 – 7 – 14 – 2. Numbers with two such factors each are not playable if they make a loop. Here the first one the player plays takes two of the four primes as tax, which leaves two for the other three.
maximal factor
A number divided by one of its primes. 12 has the maximal factors 6 and 4, 9 has only 3, and a prime has only 1. A move is legal exactly when one of its maximal factors is still in the pot.

The maximal factors of x are x/p for each prime p dividing x.

maximal-factor graph
The board as a directed graph, with an edge from each number to each of its maximal factors. Every edge leads to a smaller number, so the figures draw the edges as lines without arrowheads. Each number the player takes leaves the graph with its edges. A move is legal exactly when an edge still leads from it, and its tax is everything it still reaches along its edges.
move
A number you take. It must still have a divisor in the pot, and you score it.
node
One point of a search, where it has decided some promotions and left the rest open.
nogood
A set of candidates that the player cannot promote together from the start of a search, which the search remembers, so that no branch tries it again.
odd core
A set that passes the parity test yet is not playable, and contains no smaller such set. Every set that passes the test but is not playable contains one. The first is on the board of game 45: 12, 18, 20, 30 and 45. Odd cores are all that the search's bound misses.
opt(n)
The best score anyone can make in game n. opt(10) = 40.

opt(n) is a maximum over every legal game of n.

optimal game
A game that scores opt(n).
optimal set
The set of moves of an optimal game, and so a playable set with the largest sum. Most games have one; from game 425 on, some have two or more.

S*(n) is an optimal set of game n.

parity bound
The weight of the heaviest set that passes the parity test. Frame by frame, it limits what the promotions of a node can add; on the whole board, it bounds opt(n), and it is the prover's bound. In the games we measured, its gap above opt(n) on the whole board is about a tenth of the gap of Franklín and Moniot's bound.
parity test
A quick test that every playable set passes. List, for each number of a set, the maximal factors outside the set that could pay for it. The set fails when some of its numbers, taken together, list every factor an even number of times: 10, 14, 15 and 21 list each of 2, 3, 5 and 7 twice.
payment scheme
A choice, for each number of a set, of a maximal factor outside the set to pay for it, no two the same.
pays for
A maximal factor of a move, still in the pot when the player makes the move, pays for it. Two moves can never share a payment.
peel
The quick test of whether a set is playable. It pairs numbers with their payments one at a time by two safe steps: a number with only one maximal factor left to pay with takes it (a front move), and a factor that only one number can still use goes to that number (a back move). The set is playable exactly when peel pairs every number.
playable
A set of numbers that the player can take as moves, every one of them, in some order. {9, 15} is playable on the board of game 21: 9 first, then 15.

A set is playable iff it has a payment scheme whose precedence, which says what the player must play before what, has no cycle.

player's share
opt(n) as a share of 1 + 2 + ⋯ + n, the whole board. It tends to a limit, and the chain's best scores put it near 63%.
pot
The numbers that neither you nor the taxman has taken yet. It starts as the whole board.
prime formula
When n is an odd prime, take an optimal game of n − 1 and play n in place of the largest prime below n. The result is an optimal game of n, and the score rises by the difference of the two primes.

At a prime p, opt(p) = opt(p − 1) + p − q(p − 1), where q(p − 1) is the largest prime below p.

promote
To play one of a frame's factors as a move. A promoted factor leaves its frame and becomes a move of the frame in front, where one of its maximal factors pays for it. An optimal game of 21 promotes 9 from frame 1 into frame 0, where 3 pays for it.
promotion set
A set of candidates that the player can promote together, with every frame still able to play all of its moves.
proof kind
What proves a game's score: a search of the whole tree, the ceiling, the prime formula, a repeat, or nothing to choose. Each game's record names its own.
prover
A second program, which shares no proof code with the frame solver and proves the scores on the whole board, without frames.
repeat
A game that poses the same search problem as an earlier game, so the earlier game's answer carries over.

A repeat's answer carries over given statement U at n.

score
The sum of your moves. You beat the taxman when your score is higher than the total he collects.
search
The program's way of choosing the promotions. It tries promoting each candidate or not, and drops any branch that cannot beat the best game it has found.
search problem
What remains to choose in a game once it has its frames: the frames, the candidates and how many of them the player may promote. Two games that pose the same search problem have the same best promotions.
source
A number above n/2. It divides nothing else on the board, so no move takes it as tax. Either the player plays it, or the taxman takes it at the end. On the board of game 21 the sources are 11 to 21.

The sources of game n are the s with n/2 < s ≤ n.

statement U
The one open statement between our model of the game and the game itself. It says that some optimal game plays every number of U* with three or more prime factors. The prover proves it in every game it reaches, and it is open in general.

Statement U at n says that some optimal set of n contains every member of U* in layers 3 and up.

tax
The divisors of a move still in the pot, which the taxman takes with it. When no move remains, the taxman takes the rest of the pot too.
Taxman sequence
The best scores opt(1), opt(2), opt(3), … in order.

opt(1), opt(2), opt(3), … is A019312 in the OEIS.

top half
The numbers above n/2, the sources, and the part of the score they make. In the games that need a search, it is about 93% of the score.
U*
The sources that descending greedy keeps. In game 21, U* is 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20 and 21.
weight
The sum of a set's members, the score it earns as moves.

ΣS is the weight of a set S.
