Glossary

The words of this site, each with its meaning and an example. The groups follow the order of the story, and each idea has one name.

The game

The game, precisely

A divisor here is always a proper one, smaller than the move. opt(n) is a maximum over every legal game of n, and opt(1), opt(2), opt(3), … is A019312 in the OEIS.

game n
Taxman played on the numbers 1 to n. Game 21 is Taxman on 1 to 21. A game also means one play of it, as in "an optimal game of 21".
board
The numbers 1 to n of game n.
divisor, proper divisor
A divisor of a number divides it with nothing left over. A proper divisor is one smaller than the number itself: the proper divisors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. In Taxman, a divisor is always a proper one.
pot
The numbers that neither you nor the taxman has taken yet. It starts as the whole board.
move
A number you take. It must still have a divisor in the pot, and you score it.
tax
The divisors of a move still in the pot, which the taxman takes with it. When no move is left, the taxman takes the rest of the pot too.
score
The sum of your moves. You beat the taxman when your score is higher than the total he collects.
opt(n)
The best score anyone can make in game n. opt(10) = 40.
optimal game
A game that scores opt(n).
Taxman sequence
The best scores opt(1), opt(2), opt(3), … in order.

Sets and payments

Sets, precisely

The maximal factors of x are x/p for each prime p dividing x. A payment scheme of a set S gives each member a distinct maximal factor outside S, and a set is playable iff it has a scheme whose precedence, which says what must be played before what, has no cycle. S*(n) is an optimal set of game n, and ΣS the sum of a set S.

playable
A set of numbers that can all be taken as moves, in some order. {9, 15} is playable on the board of 21: 9 first, then 15.
optimal set
The set of moves of an optimal game: a playable set with the largest sum. Most games have one; from game 425 on, some have two or three.
weight
The sum of a set's members, the score it earns as moves. A heaviest set has the largest weight.
maximal factor
A number divided by one of its primes. 12 has the maximal factors 6 and 4, 9 has only 3, and a prime has only 1. A move is legal exactly when one of its maximal factors is still in the pot.
maximal-factor graph
The board drawn with every number joined to its maximal factors. A move is legal exactly when one of its lines leads to a number still in the pot, and its tax is everything still in the pot that it reaches by following its lines.
pays for
A maximal factor of a move, still in the pot when the move is made, pays for it. Two moves can never share a payment.
payment scheme
A choice of a maximal factor to pay for each number of a set, no two the same.
peel
The quick test of whether a set is playable. It pairs numbers with their payments one at a time by two safe steps: a number with only one maximal factor left to pay with takes it (a front move), and a factor that only one number can still use goes to that number (a back move). The set is playable exactly when every number gets paired.
loop
A number with two prime factors can be drawn as an edge between them. Such numbers can all be played exactly when their edges contain no loop. 10, 14, 15 and 21 make a loop on 2, 5, 3 and 7.
forest
A set of edges with no loop. Numbers with two prime factors, drawn as edges between their primes, can all be played exactly when they form a forest.

The chain

The chain, precisely

The ceiling is opt(n − 1) + n. At a prime p, opt(p) = opt(p − 1) + p − q(p − 1), where q(p − 1) is the largest prime below p. A repeat's answer carries over given statement U at n.

chain
Our optimal games for every n from 1 up, found in order, each built from an optimal game of the one before.
ceiling
n more than the best score of game n − 1. No game of n can beat it, so a game that reaches it is optimal.
prime formula
At a prime n, an optimal game swaps the largest prime below n for n itself, and its score rises by the difference.
repeat
A game that poses exactly the search problem of an earlier game, so the earlier game's answer carries over.
fits
Game n fits when an optimal set of game n − 1 can still be played with n added. Then game n is at its ceiling: 22 fits, and 21 does not.
search problem
What is left to choose in a game once its frames are set: the frames, the candidates and how many of them may be promoted. Two games with the same search problem have the same promotions.
cofactor
n divided by its largest prime factor: 30 = 6 · 5 has the cofactor 6. Whether a game needs a search can usually be told from it.

The top half

The top half, precisely

The sources of game n are the s with n/2 < s ≤ n, and the factors are 1 to ⌊n/2⌋. The layer of x is Ω(x), its prime factors counted with multiplicity. Statement U at n says that some optimal set of n contains every member of U* in layers 3 and up.

source
A number above n/2. It divides nothing else on the board, so it is never tax: it is a move or nothing. On the board of 21 the sources are 11 to 21.
factor
A number up to n/2. It is tax for some larger number, and sometimes a move itself.
layer
A number's count of prime factors, with repeats. 12 = 2 · 2 · 3 is in layer 3, every prime is in layer 1, and 1 alone is in layer 0.
descending greedy
The pass that chooses the sources: from the largest down, it keeps each source that can still be played with the ones kept before it.
U*
The sources that descending greedy keeps. In game 21, U* is 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20 and 21.
statement U
The one open statement between our model of the game and the game itself: that some optimal game plays every number of U* with three or more prime factors. It is proven in every game the prover reaches, and open in general.
top half
The numbers above n/2, the sources, and the part of the score they make. In the games that need a search, it is about 93% of the score.
exchange property
What makes a greedy choice safe in general: a smaller playable set can always take a member from a larger one and stay playable. The playable sets of sources lack it in game 2873.

Frames

Frames, precisely

A frame is a connected component of the graph that joins each source to its maximal factors. A frame's freedoms are its factors less its moves. A promotion set is a set of candidates that can be promoted together, keeping every frame winnable.

frame
A group of sources with every factor that can pay for them: a small game inside the big one. Game 21 has four.
in front of
One frame is in front of another when a number of the other has a maximal factor in it. Frames in front are played first, and in the figures they sit to the right.
freedom
A factor that a frame can spare: its factors less its moves. A frame with none is closed.
candidate
A factor that its frame can spare and the frame in front of it can take on. Game 21 has the candidates 4 and 9.
promote
To play a factor as a move. A promoted factor becomes a move of the frame in front. Game 21's optimal game promotes 9.
frame model
The game seen as frames, with the sources fixed to U*, so that only the promotions are left to choose. Its best score is the best score of the game wherever statement U holds.
closed
A frame with as many factors as moves, so it has none to spare. It takes no part in the search.
promotion set
A set of candidates that can all be promoted together, with every frame still able to play all of its moves.

The search

search
The program's way of choosing the promotions: it tries promoting each candidate or not, and drops any branch that cannot beat the best game found.
node
One point of a search: some promotions decided, the rest still open.
bound
An upper limit on what a node could still gain. A node whose bound cannot beat the best game found is dropped.
gap
How far a bound is above the best game found. The larger the gap, the larger the search.
branch and bound
A way of searching: split on one choice at a time, keep the best answer found, and drop every branch whose bound cannot beat it.
conflict
A few candidates that cannot be promoted together at a node. Every better set leaves one of them out, so the search branches on them.
nogood
A set of candidates that cannot be promoted together from the start of a search, which the search remembers, so that no branch tries it again.
odd core
A set that passes the parity test, which every playable set passes, yet cannot be played. The first is on the board of 45: 12, 18, 20, 30 and 45. Odd cores are all that the search's bound misses.

Trust

frame solver
The program that finds the optimal games, by searching the frame model.
prover
A second program, which shares no proof code with the frame solver and proves the scores on the whole board, without frames.
referee
The check that replays every recorded game: each move legal, the score as recorded, and no move left at the end.
verifier
A program that checks the whole file of games without changing it. There are two: the frame solver's, and one in Python that shares no code with it.
parity bound
The prover's upper bound on opt(n): the heaviest set in which no group of numbers lists every factor an even number of times. Its gap is about a tenth of Franklín and Moniot's bound's.
Franklín and Moniot's bound
An upper bound on opt(n): the heaviest set whose numbers can each be given a maximal factor of their own, from outside the set, to pay with.
proof kind
How a game of the chain was proven: by a search of the whole tree, at the ceiling, by the prime formula, as a repeat, by a bound from the bounds store, or with nothing to choose. Each game's record names its own.
bounds store
The project's file of upper bounds on opt(n) that earlier runs of the solver proved. Some games near the chain's end are proven by meeting one of its bounds.

The results

player's share
opt(n) as a share of 1 + 2 + ⋯ + n, the whole board. It tends to a constant, and the chain's games put it near 0.63.
longest game
A game with the most moves its board allows. An optimal game is usually one, but not in game 833.
