The optimal games

Every game from game 1 to game 5,060 has an optimal game, with its proof. Up to game 2,175 the proofs are complete; past it they rest on statement U, which is open (chapter 6). The best scores, opt(1), opt(2), …, are the Taxman sequence, A019312 in the OEIS.

The optimal games are in one file of 1.1 MB, results/optimal.jsonl.gz, a line for each game: the moves of its optimal game in the order played, its score and how it was proven. The explorer of chapter 7 shows any game, and its last section says what each field holds. These commands download the file and take out the moves; they need jq.

An optimal game of game 21, its moves in the order played (change 21 to any game)
curl -sL https://github.com/bvchess/taxman/raw/master/results/optimal.jsonl.gz | gzip -dc | jq -c 'select(.n == 21) | .moves'
Every game, a line each: n, its best score and the moves of an optimal game
curl -sL https://github.com/bvchess/taxman/raw/master/results/optimal.jsonl.gz | gzip -dc | jq -r '"\(.n) \(.score) \(.moves | join(" "))"' > games.txt

Game 21, step by step

Game 20's best score is 124, so game 21's is above 124 and at most its ceiling, 124 + 21 = 145 (chapter 2). 21 does not fit, is not a prime and repeats no earlier search, so game 21 needs a search. Rules settle most of the board before it, and the figure follows them one step at a time.

  1. The pot The numbers 1 to 21, none of them sorted yet.
  2. Layers Each number stands in the column of its count of prime factors, with a line to each of its maximal factors, the numbers that can pay for it (chapter 1).
  3. Sources The numbers above n/2, 11 to 21, divide nothing else on the board, so each is a move or nothing. They are the sources, and the numbers up to 10 are the factors that pay for them (chapter 3).
  4. Descending greedy From 21 down, descending greedy keeps each source still playable with those kept before it: 21, 20, 19, 18, 16, 15, 14 and 12, which score 135. It drops 17, 13 and 11, whose only maximal factor, 1, already pays for 19. By statement U, which holds here, some optimal game plays every source greedy keeps (chapter 3).
  5. Frames What is left splits into frames, each a group of sources with the factors that can pay for them (chapter 4).
  6. Freedoms The frames of layers 1 and 4 have as many factors as moves, so nothing in them can change, and they drop out. The frames of layers 2 and 3 have one freedom each, and their candidates, the factors that could be played as moves too, are 4 and 9 (chapter 4).
  7. The promotion Only one candidate fits, and 9 is the larger, so 9 is promoted: it becomes a move, paid for by 3. The search over promotions ends at its first node: 135 + 9 = 144 (chapter 4 and chapter 5).
  8. The game The game is 19, 9, 15, 21, 14, 18, 12, 20, 16, each move paid for by its tax. The player scores 144, and the taxman 87.

So game 21's best score is 144, one below its ceiling. Two programs that share no code check it with every other game, and the prover proves it again on the whole board, without statement U (chapter 6).
