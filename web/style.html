Type

The face's files

Two variable files in fonts/, upright and italic, weights 300 to 700, in the Latin subset. A symbol outside it, such as Σ, ∪, ≤ or Ω, comes from the reader's own system fonts. The site loads nothing from another site.

The site is set in Radio Canada, served from the site itself under the SIL Open Font License. Numbers are the subject of the site, so the face is chosen for its figures: a zero with no slash or dot, a 1 with a foot and a flag that no 7 can be taken for, and tabular lining figures. One custom property, --font, sets it for the text, the figures and the tables.

The scale

One family, in two weights for text (400 and 700) and one for quiet labels (600). The text is 18 px on a line of 1.62, and a phone sets it at 17 px.

Numbers in prose, in figures and in tables

The rule for numbers

Figures are lining everywhere and tabular in figures, tables, sliders and scores, so a column of numbers lines up and a changing score does not jitter. Counts take thousands separators (218,226); game numbers in running text do not need them but may have them.

Game 21 scores 144 to 87, and 17 + 71 = 88. Every game from 1 to the end of the chain has an optimal game; game 1100 scores its score, and game 833 scores its score. A number the prose names, such as 101, is set bold over a dotted line.

Color

The validator's resultdataviz validate_palette.js, on a white pagemeasured

At each tone, blue and orange pass every check, the protan and deutan simulations among them.

Precisely.The marks of each tone pass the lightness band, the chroma floor, CVD separation (deutan and protan ΔE 25 or more), the normal-vision floor and 3:1 contrast. The faces are fills under ink, not marks, and keep a deutan and protan ΔE of 9.9 or more. The page recomputes the separations from the tokens, beside each tone. A third hue would sit too close to blue for a deutan reader, so a chapter not yet written is marked by a dashed line and a gray wash.

The page is ink on white. Two colors mean something, and only two: blue is the player and the player's moves, and orange is the taxman and his tax. Every color distinction is doubled by a shape, a line style, a place or a label, since about one man in twelve does not see red from green. Blue against orange lies on the blue–yellow axis that such readers still see. What the prose names gets a halo of ink, never a third color.

Three tones

Choosing a tone

Add ?tone=deep, ?tone=light or ?tone=lightest to the address of any page to see the whole site in that tone. Any element with data-tone sets the tone inside it, as the three panels here do.

The same pair at three tones, each with real tokens in the two trays, a flat move with its tax, and a chart's lines. Lighter tones fill with a light color under an ink number and outline in a mid shade. An outline or a line in color keeps 3:1 against the page, and words in color keep 4.5:1, so in the light tones the words are ink. The site uses the lightest tone.

The tokens

The marks: one grammar, two renderings

Which rendering

Raised: the game, the loop, the game on the maximal-factor graph and the front page's game, and the tokens of the contents and the map, which are links. Flat: game 21 from the board to the frames, the three steps, the orders, and the numbers of an aside. A chapter's number in its heading is flat: it is not a thing to press.

Depth means you can touch it. A number the reader plays is a raised token, a coin for a circle and a tile for a square, standing on the page with an edge and a shadow; one that cannot be taken now lies flat. A number in a figure that explains is drawn flat, in ink. The two renderings read the same look, so each mark below means the same thing in both.

A promotion

The moment the vocabulary must make visible. Flat, the square of 9 rounds into a circle as it hops one frame forward, leaving a dashed ghost, and its payment is drawn. Raised, the tile turns over in the air and lands as a coin.

Controls

The keys

With the slider in focus, the arrow keys step from state to state, Home and End go to the first and the last, and Page Up and Page Down jump three. A figure opens at a state from its URL: ?fig21=4, ?demo=3.

A stepped figure has a time slider under it, with a named stop for each state, buttons for the state before and after, and a button that plays on. Drag the slider and the figure moves through every point between two states. Words in the prose move it too: watch 9 become a candidate, then promote it, and point at 9 or 3 to give it a halo. The raised marker on each such word names the stop it moves the figure to.

Buttons are raised, since they are pressed; the one that does the main thing is filled.

The technical layer

The right column holds everything more exact or more technical than the story, each piece beside the paragraph it belongs to. There are three kinds. A margin card states a result the story uses: its name, its standing, a plain statement and a precise one. The standing pill is the link to the proof. A technical note gives notation or an exact definition. A block of links points to the theory, the code and the papers. The standing of a card is told by its badge's outline or fill, never by color.

The frame lemmaLemma F1proved, proof on GitHub

Every number lies in exactly one frame.

Precisely.Let n ≥ 2. Every number of the board lies in exactly one component of the frame graph, whose sources lie in one layer k and factors in layer k − 1.

The length propositionProposition Q4proved by computation, proof on GitHub

In game 833 every optimal game has 362 moves, and the longest games have 363.

Precisely.The prover and a maximum matching compute it: a heaviest game of 363 moves scores 29 less than opt(833).

The chain's scoresproved given U, proof on GitHub

Every score of the chain is opt(n) wherever statement U holds at n.

Precisely.Each recorded score is ΣU* + P*, which corollary X8 makes opt(n) given U at n.

Open, proved to Nopen, proved to N

An open statement that a computation proves in every game up to N.

One optimal set, to 424measured, evidence on GitHub

Up to game 424 every game has exactly one optimal set.

Precisely.The prover enumerates the optimal sets of the games it proves; from 425 on, a few have two or three.

Statement Uopen, evidence on GitHub

Some optimal game plays every source descending greedy keeps in layers 3 and up.

Precisely.Some optimal set of n contains U*≥3.

Layers, precisely

The layer of x is Ω(x), its count of prime factors with multiplicity: Ω(12) = 3 and Ω(1) = 0.

Numbers with two prime factors can all be played exactly when their lines form no loop.

Layout: three columns, three jobs

The left column finds the reader's place, the center tells the story, and the right holds the technical layer. A reader who never looks right misses nothing of the story. Figures sit in the story's column, inline; a figure that needs more room reaches into the right column, and the notes beside it move below it. No width scrolls sideways.

  • Contents 224 px, sticky; a bar 52 px tall below a window of 1300 px.
  • Story at most 36 rem, 648 px, about 70 characters a line; a 16 px gutter on a phone.
  • Technical layer 16.5 rem, 297 px, a gutter of 2.4 rem from the story, with a hairline between.

Motion

Motion answers the reader: a figure moves when its slider, a button or a word moves it, and the page itself stays still. Flat marks glide. Raised tokens are picked up, carried and set down with a small overshoot, as a thing with weight is, and a tile turns over when it becomes a coin. With reduced motion every figure jumps.
