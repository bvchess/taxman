Notation

What the marks, colors, pills and symbols of this site mean. Every figure and every page uses the same ones.

The page

Every page has three columns. The navigation is on the left. The story is in the center, and it is written to be read on its own. The right column holds what is more exact than the story, beside the paragraph it belongs to: the precise statement of each result, technical notes, and links to the code, the data and the literature.

On a narrow screen the right column folds into the text, and each of its notes opens when you tap it.

The marks

Every figure draws numbers with the same marks. A circle is a number above n/2, or any number taken as a move; a square is a number up to n/2. The rest of each mark says what has happened to it.

Flat or raised. A number you can play stands up from the page as a token, a coin for a circle and a tile for a square. A number that cannot be taken lies flat. The numbers in a figure that explains something are drawn flat, in ink. So depth means you can touch it.

Layers and frames. A figure that sets a game out by layers puts each layer in a column, with layer 1 at the right and the highest layer at the left, so every line runs right, toward the frames that are played first. A frame is drawn as a box around its sources and its factors, and a dashed box is a closed frame.

In the text. A number in bold with a dotted underline lights up in the figure beside it when you point at it. An underlined phrase with a small tag moves the figure to the state it names.

Color

Two colors mean something, and only two. Blue is you, the player, and your moves. Orange is the taxman and his tax. Every other mark is ink and gray. Each color is backed by a shape, a place or a label too, so a reader who cannot tell the colors apart misses nothing.

Where a result stands

Each result in the right column wears a pill that says where it stands. The pill is a link to the result's proof, or to the evidence for it.

proved
The theory proves it.
proved given U
The theory proves it from statement U, which is open in general.
proved by computation
A statement about particular games, proved by running a program on them.
certified game by game
An open statement that a computation proves in each game of a range.
measured
Observed in the data, with no proof.
open
Neither proved nor refuted.

A card gives a result's name and its label in the theory, such as Lemma P1, then the result in plain words, and then, after Precisely, its exact statement.
