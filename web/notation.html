Notation

The marks, colors, pills and symbols used across the site.

The page

On a large screen each page has three columns. Navigation is on the left, the story is in the center, and the right column has more precise notes about how things work.

On a narrow screen the navigation moves to a button at the top, and notes fold into the center column where you can tap to read more.

The marks

Every figure draws numbers with the same kinds of marks. The shape around a number says what can still happen to it, its fill and outline say what has already been decided, and a color tells who took it, the player or the taxman.

A circle is a move the player could take. A square is a number that will go to the taxman either as tax or when the taxman sweeps up the remaining pot at the end of the game. A number can be taken only while one of its maximal factors is still in the pot. Numbers never come back to the pot, so once a number has none left, it can never be a move, and its circle turns into a square. The number 1 has no maximal factor, so it is always a square.

In a game you play. A circle you can take stands up from the page as a coin you can press. Every other number is drawn flat.

Layers and frames. A figure that sets a game out by layers puts each layer in a column, with layer 1 at the right and the highest layer at the left, so every line runs right, toward the frames a game plays first. In a column the larger numbers stand higher, except where a figure orders a column so that its lines run straight or do not cross. A box around sources and their factors represents a frame, and a dashed box is a closed frame. We number the frames with freedoms from the front. Frame 0 is at the right, frame 1 is behind it, and so on, and we collapse frames at the same distance from the front into one. A dotted line joins a candidate to each of its maximal factors in the frame in front, and only a promotion, the candidate's hop into that frame, wears an arrowhead.

In the text. A number in bold with a dotted underline lights up in the figure beside it when you point at it.

Color

Blue represents the player's moves. Orange goes to the taxman. Green is for the search, the computer's work that proves a game: the games a search settled, and what each search cost.

Standings

Each result in the right column wears a pill that explains where it stands. The pill is a link to the result's proof, or to the evidence for it.

proved
The theory proves it.
conditional
The theory proves it from statement U, which is open in general.
proved by computation
A statement about particular games, which we prove by running a program on them.
open, proved to game N
An open statement that a computation proves in every game up to game N.
measured
The data show it, with no proof.
open
No one has proved or refuted it.

A card gives a result's name and its label in the theory, such as Lemma P1, then the result in plain words, and below a thin line its exact statement.
