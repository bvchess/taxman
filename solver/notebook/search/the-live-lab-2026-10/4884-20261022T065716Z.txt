At the event (06:31:08Z) game 4884 had 172.6e6 steps, best promotion sum 543,308 and 3,600 s of own search. It was

I spent the session on the open request, the gate of conflict branching (a check uses the tactic only where its gap is
at least `CONFLICT_BRANCHING_GAP` = 300). For that I built a variant on the local branch `study-4884-gate` (commit
4f74667), where the environment variable `TAXMAN_CB_GAP` sets the gate when the feature `studies` is on. The build is in
`work/target-gate`; the lab's own `tune` is unchanged. Findings:

* By steps, a lower gate always wins: gate 0 takes about half the steps of gate 300.
* By CPU, gate 0 loses: a step costs about twice the CPU. The best gate by CPU lies between 150 and 300, on this game's
  units and on whole games from 1000 to 3000.
* Nothing measured here argues for a gate that depends on the size of the game.

  gives 5.8e5 steps. The 4880 fit (0.605 + 0.391·gap/100) gives 1.36e6. At 52k steps/s that is 11 to 26 s.

## Ideas tried

1. **Conflict branching on against off on units just above the gate (the request).** On the 17 units of gap 300 to 571:
   * Per unit, the rule cost steps on 9, saved on 7 and tied on 1. The median ratio on/off is 1.4, and the worst case
     costs 43 times as many steps (4884-1474: 1,535 against 36).
   * The losses are small in absolute terms, under 1,500 steps each. The gains are large: 4884-1134 took 1,811 against
     14,100, and 4884-591 took 293 against 3,185.
   * In total the rule took 0.31 of off's steps and 0.54 of its CPU.
   * On the large units (gap over 1,000), on finished 2 of 4 in 120 s and off finished none. Where off was stopped, on
     had searched past it.

   So the rule behaves like insurance: a constant-factor cost on typical small subtrees, and large savings on the
   heavy tail.
2. **The gate's value (0, 150, 300, 450, 600, 800) on the same units and on whole games 1000 to 3000.**
   * Steps fall steadily as the gate drops, but CPU per step rises. At gate 0 a step costs about twice the CPU (225 to
     272 steps/s on the large units against 508 to 554 at gate 300).
   * By CPU, gate 300 is best on the large units of 4884 (82 to 87 s against 97 to 110 at gates 150 and 0, and over
     117 at 450).
   * Gates 150 and 300 tie on the mid units and on games 1500 and 2000. Gate 150 is 12% cheaper on game 3000 (36.5 s
     against 41.3 s).
   * Gate 450 and above is worse everywhere.
   * The best gate does not drift with game size from 1000 to 3000 or on the units of 4884.

   I could not reproduce the request's cost on game 1000, for a reason worth knowing. Proving against the known
   optimum, no check of game 1000 reaches gap 300, so gate 300 equals off (1,734 steps each). The cost the request cites
   (3,360 against 2,026) comes from a search from scratch with a weak incumbent, where gaps are larger. **So the cost
   lies in the early phase of a search, before the incumbent is good, and not in the gate as a function of the game's
   size.**

## Open questions

* Is gate 150 better than 300 on large games? It was 12% cheaper on game 3000 and about even elsewhere. A whole-game
  test on two or three games of 3000 to 4000 would settle it, with the CPU noise controlled by running fewer at a time.
* Does game 1000's cost from scratch come from checks made while the incumbent is still weak? A gate tied to the
  incumbent's age, or to how far the incumbent is below the bound, might be what the request is after. This needs
  `taxman solve` built from the branch, which I did not do.
* Why does a step at gate 0 cost twice the CPU? The likely cause is choosing the conflict at every check (a GF(2)
  pricing per member). A cheaper pricing would make the low gate's halving of steps a real saving.
