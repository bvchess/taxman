## Ideas tried

* **Conflict branching on the old search's units:** the only lever measured.  On the units that finish, its advantage
  over the default grows with the gap.  Steps on/off: 0.87 (gap 900), 0.41 (800), 0.30 (1,100), 0.093 (1,000).  CPU
  on/off: 1.25, 0.97, 0.54, 0.21.  This agrees with the notebook's 0.1 to 0.03 on fresh units of gap 931 to 1,166.
* **Restart against switching the running search to conflict branching:** a switch applies only to units started
  after it, and the old deque holds 14,230 deep units.  Their sum (3 × 10⁸ to 1.5 × 10⁹) is 2 to 8 times the fresh
  search's.  So a restart beats a switch, as well as beating staying the course.
* **A better incumbent:** none to find.  The finder reached 538080 in 1.3 s, and it is the proven optimum.
* Nothing from the register's closed avenues was retried.

## Open questions

* `tune` has no `--cpu-limit`, though the study procedure and the request assume it.  Either add it, or have the
  procedure speak of `--node-limit`; I used step limits sized by a guessed step cost, which came out three to five
  times longer than 120 s.
* Units of an old, deep deque scatter widely around any gap line: 624 against 29,421 steps 100 points apart.  Gap
  alone predicts them poorly.  Depth or the cheapest member's price may explain more; that has not been measured.
* My fresh search ran 3 to 5 times slower per step than the notebook's laptop figure.  It shared the machine with 14
  unit runs and possibly the chain's 72 threads.  Step costs measured under load should not be compared with unloaded
  ones.
